A word about “decision fatigue”

You’ve probably called this decision fatigue. The popular version, where willpower is a battery that runs flat after too many choices, has had a rough decade. When 23 labs tried to replicate it, the effect was small enough that it might not exist at all.

What the science backs is not very surprising to those of us with ADHD: decisions you haven’t closed keep running in the background.

Why your brain won’t let it go

An open decision behaves like an unfinished task. It pings you at inconvenient moments because, as far as your brain is concerned, nothing has been handled.

Open decision
Plan made
Masicampo & Baumeister

Psychologists E.J. Masicampo and Roy Baumeister found that making a specific plan for an unfinished goal reduced those intrusive thoughts, even before people acted on the plan.

Apparently a credible plan is enough for your brain to stop nagging.

What a closed decision looks like

A closed decision has four parts:

01

What you’re really choosing between (often not the options you started with)

02

Why you chose what you chose, written down

03

What happens next, and when

04

What new information would justify reopening it

No. 4 is carrying a lot of weight here; we need to decide in advance what would count as a real reason to reopen the decision:

Counts
  • a client cancels
  • the numbers come in under X
Doesn’t count
  • Doubt turning up on a Tuesday afternoon
  • the 2am spiral
  • a competitor’s launch post
  • your friend saying “hmm, are you sure?”
(If you know, you know.)
