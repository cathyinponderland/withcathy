The science

Why your brain won’t let go

You’ve probably heard that you have a limited willpower battery that runs out by evening. That idea has taken a hit: when 23 labs tried to replicate it, the effect came out small, possibly nonexistent.

What holds up better is this: an open decision behaves like an unfinished task. It keeps running in the background and interrupting you. In Masicampo and Baumeister’s research, simply making a specific plan for an unfinished goal reduced those intrusive thoughts, even before people acted on it.

So the fix isn’t more willpower. It’s closing decisions properly.

What a closed decision looks like

A closed decision has four parts:

01

What you’re really choosing between. Often not the options you started with.

02

Why you chose it, written down, so you can explain it to leadership, your team or future you.

03

What happens next, and when.

04

What new information would justify reopening it.

Reasons that count for reopening
  • A key client cancels
  • the numbers come in under a threshold you set in advance
  • leadership changes the brief
Reasons that don’t
  • Tuesday-afternoon doubt
  • the 2am spiral
  • a competitor’s launch post
  • someone asking “are you sure?”

No decision can guarantee an outcome. But you can be certain your reasoning holds up.
